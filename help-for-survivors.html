Support and safety

Help for Victim-Survivors

This page focuses on practical steps a victim-survivor can take after threats, exposure, or suspected distribution of intimate images. It does not replace legal advice, counseling, or emergency help.

Preserve Evidence

Save screenshots, URLs, usernames, dates, messages, threats, platform names, and any evidence showing who posted or threatened to post the images. Avoid deleting messages before documenting them.

Report and Request Removal

Report the content to the platform and use any nonconsensual intimate image, harassment, copyright, or privacy reporting tools available. Cox (2018) shows that platform response can be uneven, so documentation matters.

Tell a Trusted Person

Wolak and Finkelhor (2016) found that many victims did not seek help because of shame or fear of judgment. A trusted friend, family member, school official, counselor, or advocate can help preserve evidence and reduce isolation.

Safety Plan

Digital Safety

Change passwords, turn on two-factor authentication, review connected devices, check cloud storage, and lock down social media privacy settings.

Legal/Institutional Help

Consider contacting local law enforcement, a school or workplace Title IX/harassment office, a victim advocate, or a legal resource such as Without My Consent.

Emotional Support

Victim-survivors may experience fear, shame, anxiety, and trauma. Support from counseling services, crisis resources, and trusted people can help reduce the isolation created by the offender.
