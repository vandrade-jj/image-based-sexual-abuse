Legal response

Laws

Nonconsensual distribution of intimate images can involve criminal, civil, and platform-based responses. Laws help, but they can be difficult to apply because images move quickly, offenders may hide behind anonymous accounts, and incidents may involve multiple jurisdictions.

New York Law Example

  • New York criminalizes the unlawful dissemination or publication of an intimate image.
  • The law focuses on intentionally sharing an intimate image of an identifiable person without that person’s consent.
  • The depicted person must have had a reasonable expectation that the image would remain private.

State Law Lookup

  • People can look up laws and civil options in their own state using the Without My Consent 50 State Project.
  • Without My Consent: 50 State Project
  • The New York page is useful here as an example, but victims should check their own state because laws vary.

Are Current Laws Sufficient?

  • Current laws are helpful, but they are not always sufficient.
  • Problems include inconsistent legal definitions, anonymous accounts, deleted evidence, platform delays, and jurisdiction issues.
  • Consent can also be misunderstood: consent to create or privately send an image is not consent to distribute it.

Acts and Intention

Acts

  • Posting, publishing, sending, trading, forwarding, or threatening to share a private intimate image without consent.
  • Using social media, messaging apps, private groups, cloud storage, or websites to spread the image.
  • Continuing to share or repost content after the victim asks for it to stop.

Intention

  • The offender may act intentionally, knowingly, or with the purpose of harming, threatening, harassing, humiliating, coercing, or controlling the victim.
  • Intent can be difficult to prove when the offender uses fake accounts, deletes messages, or claims the image was shared by someone else.
  • Digital evidence such as messages, usernames, timestamps, screenshots, and URLs can help show what happened and why.

Key legal challenge: Proving who posted the image, what they intended, and where the offense legally occurred can be difficult when the victim, offender, platform, and viewers are in different locations.
