The legal landscape

Laws and Consequences.

New York is one example, but laws differ by state. Visitors should use state-specific resources and contact local authorities or legal help.

§ 01

State-law lookup

  • Visitors can look up their own state using the Without My Consent 50 State Project.
  • This matters because legal definitions, penalties, civil claims, and reporting options vary by state.
  • New York is used here only as an example; a victim should check the law where they live and report to local police if they believe a crime occurred.

§ 02

New York example

  • New York criminalizes unlawful dissemination or publication of an intimate image.
  • The core issue is whether a private intimate image of an identifiable person was shared without consent.
  • Civil remedies may also be possible depending on the facts, including privacy-related claims, intentional infliction of emotional distress, conspiracy, or conversion.

Acts Needed to Prove the Crime

  • The offender distributed, posted, shared, published, traded, or threatened to publish an intimate image.
  • The image showed an identifiable person.
  • The image was private or intimate in nature.
  • The victim did not consent to distribution, even if the image was originally made or shared privately with consent.

Intention Needed to Prove the Crime

  • The offender acted intentionally or knowingly, depending on the law used.
  • The offender knew, or should have understood, that the victim did not consent to distribution.
  • Some laws may require proof that the offender intended to harm, harass, threaten, coerce, or cause emotional distress.
  • Intention can be difficult to prove when the offender uses anonymous accounts, deletes messages, or claims someone else posted the image.

§ 03

Are current laws sufficient?

  • Current laws are helpful because they recognize that private image distribution without consent is a serious harm.
  • They are not fully sufficient because laws vary by state and online abuse can cross multiple jurisdictions.
  • Evidence can disappear if posts, accounts, URLs, or messages are deleted before documentation.
  • Anonymous usernames, shared devices, overseas platforms, and private groups can make it difficult to identify the offender.
