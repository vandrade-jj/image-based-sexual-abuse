Legal response

Laws

Nonconsensual distribution of intimate images can involve criminal, civil, and platform-based responses. However, laws can be difficult to apply because images move quickly, offenders may use anonymous accounts, and incidents may involve multiple jurisdictions.

New York Law

New York criminalizes the unlawful dissemination or publication of an intimate image. The core idea is that a person may not intentionally share an intimate image of another identifiable person when the depicted person had a reasonable expectation of privacy and did not consent to the distribution.

Civil Remedies

Without My Consent explains that victims may also consider civil claims connected to nonconsensual publication, including intentional infliction of emotional distress, negligent infliction of emotional distress, conspiracy, conversion, or related privacy-based claims depending on the facts.

Are Current Laws Sufficient?

Current laws help, but they are not always sufficient. Shapiro (2023) notes that inconsistent definitions, jurisdictional issues, proof problems, and motive requirements can make detection, prosecution, and accountability difficult.

Mens Rea and Actus Reus

Actus Reus

The prohibited act is the distribution, posting, sharing, publication, or threatened publication of a private intimate image without the depicted person's consent.

Mens Rea

The required mental state often involves acting intentionally, knowingly, or with intent to harm, harass, threaten, coerce, or cause distress, depending on the statute used.

Key legal challenge: Consent to create or privately send an image is not the same as consent to distribute it. Proving who posted the image, what they intended, and where the offense legally occurred can be difficult when platforms, accounts, and victims are in different places.
