Cyberpredator profile

The Nonconsensual Image Distributor.

This offender uses private images as leverage, entertainment, revenge, or control.

§ 01

Typical demographics

  • Official statistics are limited because many offenders are anonymous, unidentified, or prosecuted under different state laws.
  • Research summarized by Shapiro (2023) most often identifies offenders as male adolescents and adults.
  • Victim reports show that offenders may be current partners, ex-partners, friends, acquaintances, online contacts, or unknown users.

§ 02

Typical motives

  • Revenge: punishing a person after a breakup, rejection, or conflict.
  • Control: pressuring the victim to reconcile, stay silent, send more images, or obey demands.
  • Humiliation: damaging the victim’s reputation among friends, family, classmates, employers, or online audiences.
  • Status or entertainment: trading images in private groups for attention, access, or approval from others.

§ 03

Methods that work and why

1. Exploiting trust

  • Images may begin in a romantic or sexual relationship.
  • The offender later converts private trust into leverage.
  • Victims may fear shame or blame if others see the image.

2. Using private platforms

  • Closed groups and chat spaces make abuse harder to see.
  • Cox (2018) describes nonconsensual images being traded in Slack workspaces.
  • Users may migrate to new platforms when older spaces are removed.

3. Threatening exposure

  • Offenders threaten to send images to parents, schools, employers, or followers.
  • The threat works because public exposure can cause fear and panic.
  • Anonymous accounts make it harder for victims to know who is responsible.
