Cyberpredator profile

Nonconsensual Image Distributors

Nonconsensual image distributors are offenders who share, post, trade, or threaten to expose intimate images without permission. Some offenders personally know the victim; others operate through online communities, private chats, or anonymous accounts.

Common Demographics

Shapiro (2023) explains that available demographic data is limited because offenders are difficult to identify and laws vary by jurisdiction. However, research most often identifies offenders as heterosexual male adolescents and adults. The data should be read carefully because some racial findings may reflect the samples used rather than the entire offender population.

Typical Motives

Offenders may act for revenge, humiliation, control, harassment, sexual gratification, social status, entertainment, or financial gain. In abusive relationships, the image becomes a tool for coercion: the offender uses exposure to punish, pressure, or silence the victim-survivor.

Common Methods

Images may come from a past relationship, stolen accounts or devices, screenshots, cloud storage, coercion, or private messages. Cox (2018) shows how offenders can also move into closed platforms and private workspaces where users trade images and require others to contribute images to gain access.

Why These Methods Work

Trust is exploited

Many images begin in a romantic or sexual relationship. Consent to create or privately share an image does not mean consent to distribute it later.

Digital spread is fast

Images can be copied, saved, reposted, or moved across platforms before the victim-survivor has time to respond.

Anonymity protects offenders

Anonymous accounts, private groups, and cross-platform movement make it harder to identify the person who uploaded or circulated the image.
