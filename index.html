A focused form of image-based sexual abuse

Nonconsensual
Distribution of
Intimate Images

This project examines the sharing, posting, or threatening to share private sexual images without consent. The crime is not about a victim's choice to trust someone; it is about an offender using digital access, power, and exposure to harm another person.

Explore Research

Crime Definition

  • Nonconsensual distribution of intimate images occurs when nude, sexual, or intimate images are shared, posted, traded, or threatened to be shared without the depicted person's permission.
  • It is one specific form of image-based sexual abuse.
  • Consent to create or privately share an image is not the same as consent to distribute it publicly or send it to others.

General Information

  • Offenders may be current partners, ex-partners, acquaintances, online contacts, or anonymous users in private digital groups.
  • Phones, cloud storage, social media, messaging apps, and private online communities make images easier to copy and circulate.
  • Once an image spreads, victim-survivors may lose control over where it appears, who sees it, and how long it remains online.

Prevalence and Statistics

  • Research summarized by Shapiro (2023) reports prevalence ranges of 1%–30% for nonconsensual distribution.
  • Threats to distribute intimate images have been reported in prevalence ranges of 1%–15%.
  • Underreporting is likely because victims may experience stigma, self-blame, fear, or delayed discovery.
