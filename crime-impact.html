Consequences of the crime

Crime Impact

Nonconsensual distribution of intimate images can cause harm long after the original post or threat. The damage often continues because digital images can be copied, reposted, searched, and shared in new spaces.

Impact on Victims and Families

  • Victim-survivors may experience fear, humiliation, anxiety, depression, shame, loss of trust, and reputational harm.
  • Wolak and Finkelhor (2016) reported that 24% of sextortion survey respondents saw a medical or mental health practitioner and 12% had to move because of the incident.
  • Victims may worry that images will be reposted, sent to family, shown at school, shared with employers, or used again as leverage.
  • Families can be affected when offenders threaten to expose images to parents, relatives, partners, schools, or workplaces.
  • The crisis may create embarrassment, conflict, fear, and pressure for the victim-survivor to manage the abuse alone.

Impact on Society

  • The crime normalizes sexual humiliation, private-image trading, and victim-blaming.
  • It can make online spaces feel unsafe, especially for adolescents and young adults who rely on social media, texting, and private messaging.
  • Cox (2018) shows that nonconsensual images can be traded in closed digital spaces where users request, exchange, and verify access to images.
  • The spread of images creates pressure on platforms, schools, workplaces, law enforcement, and communities to respond quickly and responsibly.
  • When people forward, save, laugh at, or request private images, they contribute to the harm even if they were not the original offender.

Why the Harm Continues

  • Images can move from one platform to another before the victim knows they were shared.
  • Anonymous accounts and private groups make it harder to identify every person involved.
  • Removal from one website does not guarantee that copies are gone everywhere.
  • The victim-survivor may feel a long-term loss of control because the image can resurface later.
