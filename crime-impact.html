Consequences of the crime

Crime Impact

Nonconsensual distribution of intimate images can cause harm long after the original post or threat. The damage continues because digital images can be copied, reposted, searched, and shared in new spaces.

Impact on Victims

  • Victim-survivors may experience fear, humiliation, anxiety, depression, shame, and loss of trust.
  • They may fear that images will resurface at school, work, home, or in future relationships.
  • Wolak and Finkelhor (2016) reported that 24% of surveyed sextortion victims saw a medical or mental health practitioner.
  • Wolak and Finkelhor (2016) also reported that 12% had to move because of the incident.

Impact on Families

  • Families may be pulled into the abuse when offenders threaten to send images to parents, relatives, partners, schools, or employers.
  • The incident can create fear, embarrassment, conflict, and pressure on the victim-survivor to stay silent.
  • Family members may also struggle with how to support the victim while preserving evidence and responding safely.

Impact on Society

  • The crime normalizes sexual humiliation, privacy violations, victim-blaming, and image trading.
  • Private online groups can turn abuse into a social exchange where users request, trade, and verify access to nonconsensual images.
  • Schools, workplaces, platforms, police, and communities face pressure to respond to harms that can spread across many online spaces.

Why the Harm Spreads

  • Images can be moved from one platform to another, stored by unknown users, or reposted after removal.
  • Closed digital communities can make the abuse harder to detect and harder to stop.
  • The victim-survivor’s loss of control can continue even after the first post is deleted.
  • The social impact is larger than one victim because it teaches others that privacy violations can be used as entertainment, punishment, or control.
