Consequences of the crime

Crime Impact

Nonconsensual distribution of intimate images can cause harm long after the original post or threat. The damage often continues because digital images can be copied, reposted, searched, and shared in new spaces.

Impact on Victims

Victim-survivors may experience fear, humiliation, anxiety, depression, loss of trust, reputational damage, and fear that the images will resurface. Wolak and Finkelhor (2016) reported that 24% of respondents saw a medical or mental health practitioner and 12% had to move as a result of sextortion.

Impact on Families

Families may be pulled into the crisis when offenders threaten to send images to parents, relatives, partners, schools, or workplaces. This can create fear, conflict, embarrassment, and pressure on the victim-survivor to manage the harm alone.

Impact on Society

The crime normalizes sexual humiliation, private-image trading, and victim-blaming. Cox (2018) shows how private groups can turn abuse into a social exchange where users request, trade, and verify access to nonconsensual images.

Why the Harm Spreads

The online setting makes the harm broader than one incident. Images can be moved from one platform to another, stored by unknown users, or reposted after removal. This makes the victim-survivor's loss of control ongoing and creates pressure on platforms, schools, workplaces, law enforcement, and communities to respond more effectively.
